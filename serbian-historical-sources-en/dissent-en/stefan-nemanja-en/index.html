Stefan Nemanja and religious persecution

The medieval witness

The Life of Saint Simeon, written by his son Stefan the First-Crowned in the early thirteenth century, depicts suppression of heresy as a royal virtue. It describes mutilation, punishment, expulsion and destruction of books. The text does not explicitly name Bogomils; this identification is a historiographical interpretation.

Comparison with Saint Sava

Roach contrasts Nemanja’s repression with the greater emphasis on preaching and reconversion in the account of the assembly at Žiča in 1221. These hagiographies express the authorities’ perspective, not that of the persecuted.

Andrew P. Roach (2006), The competition for souls: Sava of Serbia and consumer choice in religion in the thirteenth century Balkans, Glasnik 50(1)

I. Komatina, Initial 11 (2023), pp. 93–114
