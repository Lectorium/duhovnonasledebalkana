Historical Research

The essays in this section reconstruct Balkan spiritual movements through their sources and political contexts. Each contribution distinguishes documentary evidence from scholarly hypotheses and contemporary reflection.

Essays

The Bogomils: history, sources, and religious dissent

An introductory essay on the dualist movement attested in the medieval Balkans. It examines the testimony of Cosmas the Priest, doctrines attributed to the Bogomils, ecclesiastical condemnations, and relations with other dualist communities. It distinguishes what the sources attest from a contemporary reading of freedom of conscience.

Medieval Serbia: Nemanja, Orthodoxy, and dissent

What do Serbian sources say about Stefan Nemanja’s measures against heresy? An analysis of the Life of Simeon, its dynastic and religious character, the debated identification of the accused as Bogomils, and the relationship between ecclesiastical autonomy and religious freedom.

The Bosnian Church and the debate on dualism

An account of the debate about the Bosnian Church: what do contacts with dualist communities demonstrate? Why are they insufficient to define the entire community as Bogomil or to explain subsequent conversions to Islam in a linear way?

How to read these contributions

Medieval sources about dissenters were often written by their opponents. A religious condemnation therefore tells us both about the ideas being challenged and about the aims of those who formulated it. The essays indicate when a reconstruction is documented, when scholars debate it, and when a philosophical interpretation is being offered.

The guiding theme is the relationship between faith, authority, and conscience. Comparisons between different periods are not presented as a direct genealogy: they help us ask questions about dissent and dignity without attributing a single spiritual identity to entire peoples.
