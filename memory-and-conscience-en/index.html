Memory and Conscience

The memory of conflict is not a single story. This section gathers perspectives on recent Serbian history and the relationship between collective identity, dissent, and individual conscience.

Civilians’ experiences cannot be reduced to the decisions of governments, political movements, or armed forces. Acknowledging the suffering of one community does not erase that of another; documenting crimes does not mean assigning collective guilt to a people. The contributions therefore distinguish actors, responsibilities, testimonies, and moral judgements.

An account of memories of the Kosovo war and the 1999 NATO bombing, documented responsibilities in the conflict, and civil dissent in Serbia. The text keeps episodes, sources, and actors distinct, avoiding both exclusive victimhood and collective blame.

Conscience as inner freedom

A reflection on inner responsibility: distinguishing personal inquiry from automatic obedience and asking how to defend one’s dignity without denying the dignity of others. This is a contemporary philosophical text, not a reconstruction of Bogomil ideas.

A comparison across periods, without invented continuity

Comparison with medieval religious dissent is a reflection on conscience and power. It does not assert a direct historical descent between the Bogomils and modern Serbia, nor does it attribute the contemporary language of rights to historical actors. These are different experiences placed in dialogue through a shared question about dignity and obedience.

Serbian society, like other societies in the region, is plural. Memories of conflict, political positions, and personal experiences do not coincide. The project therefore considers internal dissent as well and distinguishes citizens’ choices from those of institutions.
